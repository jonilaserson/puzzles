Puzzles

small mathematical puzzles, with proofs and playable simulators

  • 01 Emptying a pole with doubling disc transfers Three poles hold discs. You may move discs between poles, but only in the exact amount that doubles the receiving pole. Show that from any start, you can always empty one pole. number theory · binary
  • 02 Fifteen balls always end at 1·2·3·4·5 Split 15 balls into any groups you like. Each move, take one ball from every group and pile them up as a new group. However you start, you always get trapped at (1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Prove it. bulgarian solitaire · monovariant
  • 03 The frog you can hear but cannot see A frog hides on one of ten poles in a dark swamp — you hear it, you can't see it. Every shot that misses, it hops to a neighboring pole. How many bullets guarantee the kill? pursuit · parity
  • 04 Every two cards share exactly one shape The one rule of Dobble. With k shapes on every card, how many cards can one deck hold — and how many shapes does it take? finite geometry · duality
