Take the last stone —
if the pile lets you

One pile, alternating takes, and a single rule that makes every take cap the next. Most piles fall to careful play. Some sizes cannot be beaten.

The game. A pile of n stones. You and the site take turns removing stones; whoever takes the last stone wins. The opening take can be any number of stones — just not the whole pile. Every later take is at least 1 and at most twice the take before it.

The puzzle. Which pile sizes can the opener always win, and how? Which piles are lost the moment the game starts?

Play below — the site never blunders. Win a few piles as opener, and note the sizes where you can't, before flipping to the proof.

The game is Fibonacci Nim, first analysed by Michael Whinihan (1963); the winning strategy runs on Zeckendorf's theorem.
