The frog you can hear
but cannot see

Ten poles in a dark swamp, a frog on one of them. Every shot that misses, it hops one pole over. How many bullets do you need to be sure?

The setup. Ten poles stand in a swamp. On one of them sits a frog. It is night: you can hear it, but you can't see it.

The move. Shoot any pole. If the frog is there, the croaking stops — you win. If not, it silently hops to a pole right next to its own, and you shoot again.

The puzzle. Find a way of shooting that is certain to get the frog — with as few bullets as possible.

01 Hunt every frog at once

You never find out where the frog is. So keep every pole it could be on, and shoot until none are left.

02 Unroll time

Give every pole a column and every turn a row. Then a whole life of the frog is one line, and you can see all of it at once.

03 The frog is a bishop

Colour the board like a chessboard. Every step the frog takes is diagonal, so it lands on its own colour every time. Steer it yourself and try to reach the other colour.

So there are only two frogs to catch — one on the dark squares, one on the light. And a square you shoot sits on one colour, so it can only ever catch one of them.

04 A wall the dark frog cannot pass

Shoot pole 2, then 3, then 4, up to 9: a wall, every square of it dark. A wall can only be passed from the doorway beside it — and try standing in one.

The square beside a dark square is light. The dark frog can never stand in a doorway; the light one strolls through.

05 Now the same wall, backwards

So aim the other eight bullets at the light frog: shoot 9 down to 2. A second wall — every square light, every doorway dark.

07 Why not fifteen?

Eight of those bullets are dark and eight are light, and neither colour can borrow from the other. Try to beat it: click squares to build your own plan.

08 Fewer poles, more poles
