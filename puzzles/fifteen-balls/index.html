bulgarian solitaire · monovariants · a visual proof

Fifteen balls always
end at 1·2·3·4·5

A visual proof. Self-contained. Nothing is ever sorted.

The game. 15 balls, in groups. One move: take one ball from each group. The taken balls form a new group. Empty groups disappear.

The claim. Every start reaches the groups (1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Then nothing ever changes.

01 The board

Put the groups in rows of boxes. Any order. Keep that order forever.

Push each row against a left wall. A row is a solid run: no holes inside.

The corner = the top-left cell. Rows and columns are counted from it.

Depth of a cell = row + column − 1.

02 The move, drawn

Peel the left column: one ball per living row.

Lay it on top, keeping positions: the ball from row r goes to position r.

No other ball moves.

The corner's (row + column) is unchanged. So every standing ball keeps its depth.

The peeled ball from row r had depth r. It lands at cell (1, r). Depth r again.

Fact 1. If the pasted row has no gap, no ball changes its depth.

03 Gaps cost depth

A row can die. Its place remains: a dead row.

Peel past a dead row, and the pasted row has a gap.

A group must be a solid run. So close the gap: every ball past it slides one step left. Depth −1 each.

Total depth = the sum of all 15 balls' depths.

Fact 2. No gap: total depth unchanged. Closing gaps: total depth drops by at least 1.

Total depth is a positive whole number. It cannot drop forever.

So after some move: no gap ever closes again.

04 Sorted, without sorting

Suppose the rows are not sorted: some row is shorter than a row below it.

Then some column holds a hole above a ball.

At the wall, that column is peeled. The pasted row gets a gap. A drop.

A dead row above a living one: the same story, already in column 1.

But drops have ended. So neither can ever happen again:

From here on, rows are sorted — largest on top — and dead rows sit only at the bottom. Forever. Not by sorting. By survival.

05 Every line is a ring

Take line 5 of such a state. Its 5 cells are fixed places: slots. Slot 1 = its top-row cell. Slot 5 = its wall cell.

A slot holding a ball is full. A slot without one is a hole.

One move. A standing ball keeps its cell — but the corner moved up-right. Measured from the corner: row +1, column −1. The next slot of its own line.

The wall ball (slot d) is peeled. It lands at cell (1, d). Slot 1.

Balls shift one slot forward. Holes shift with them.

06 Ball meets hole

Each ball — and each hole — now stays on its ring forever, circling one slot per move.

Take a ball on line d+1. Suppose line d is not full: somewhere on its ring sits a hole.

The ball surfaces — visits its top-row cell (1, d+1) — every d+1 moves. Between two surfacings, the hole's ring of d slots clicks d+1 times: its own lap, plus one. Each surfacing, the hole stands one slot further along its ring.

So within d surfacings they surface together: the ball at (1, d+1), the hole at (1, d). A top row that reaches column d+1 and skips column d. A hole inside a solid run.

Rows are solid. This never happens. There was no hole.

Fact 3. A ball on line d+1 forces line d to be completely full.

07 The count

Let D = the deepest line holding a ball.

Fact 3, chained downward: lines 1 … D−1 are all full.

Full lines hold 1+2+⋯+(D−1) balls. Line D holds between 1 and D.

D = 4: at most 1+2+3 + 4 = 10. Too few.

D = 6: at least 1+2+3+4+5 + 1 = 16. Too many.

D = 5: line 5 holds 15 − 10 = 5. Full.

Every game: finitely many gap closures → rows sorted forever → pure clicks → the staircase → frozen. ∎

08 Why 15

15 = 1+2+3+4+5. Used exactly once: in the count.

With 14 balls: lines 1–4 full, and 4 balls circling the 5-ring forever. A cycle. No rest.

A resting state exists ⇔ the ball count is 1+2+⋯+k.

09 Playground

Any start, any order — or type your own, with any number of balls. Boxes never move. New rows pile on top; the wall eats columns from the left.

The game is known as Bulgarian solitaire. Theorem: J. Brandt (1982); popularized by Martin Gardner (1983). The no-sorting formulation and the gap monovariant in this page are due to Jonathan.
