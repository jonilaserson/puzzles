conway's soldiers · peg jumps · a visual proof

An endless army,
and a line to cross

Below the line, a soldier on every square, forever. One kind of jump moves them. Each row above the line is harder to reach than the last — climb as high as you can.

The game. Below the line stands an endless army: one soldier on every square. A soldier may jump horizontally or vertically over a soldier right next to it, landing on the empty square just beyond — and the soldier jumped over leaves the board.

The puzzle. Send a soldier as high above the line as you can. You may use as many soldiers and as many jumps as you like. How high can a scout get?

Row 1 is a warm-up; row 2 takes planning. Climb below before flipping to the proof.

Conway's Soldiers, devised by John Conway (1961). The proof of how high a scout can get — and no higher — is his.
